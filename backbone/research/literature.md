# Learned time-to-goal for SM64 search: literature review and recommendations

Scope: temporal-distance / goal-conditioned value learning, learned search heuristics, set-based state encoders, prior SM64/TAS ML, and how these apply to our IQE distance head used inside objective-RRT and weighted-A* over savestates. Marks: **[verified]** = checked against the abstract or paper page; **[from memory]** = my recollection of the paper body, not re-read for this review; **[unsure]** = I am not confident of the detail.

## Executive summary (ranked by expected impact)

1. **Replace min-of-MC-labels with Bellman/TD consistency on short transitions.** Long-range Spearman ~0.2 is the textbook failure of Monte-Carlo hindsight labels on suboptimal data: the labels measure *the route the tree took*, not the shortest route. Offline GCRL methods that "stitch" short segments into long ones (HIQL's action-free IQL value, QRL, TMD, multistep quasimetric learning) all rely on local one-step or n-step constraints plus a quasimetric or bootstrapped target, never on long MC labels alone. Add `d(s,g) <= k + d(s_k,g)` with an expectile (tau around 0.9) on the TD target. This is an in-sample soft-min over all tree branches leaving `s`, so information from *every* tree feeds every state's distance.
2. **Use multistep (n-step) targets, not 1-step.** Horizon is the main driver of poor offline-RL scaling ([Park et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04168)). Multistep MC plus quasimetric training reaches 4,000-step tasks ([Zheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07730)). For us: `target = min(MC_upper_bound, n_frames + d_target(s_{t+n}, g))`, with n of 30-300 frames, a target network (EMA), and the exact frame count as cost.
3. **Keep the quasimetric (IQE) architecture and lean on the triangle inequality for horizon generalization.** Quasimetric parameterizations generalize from near goals to far goals ([Myers et al. 2025, horizon generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02709)). TMD distills optimal distances from suboptimal data by enforcing invariances through the architecture ([Myers et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20478)). TMD is the closest published recipe to what we need.
4. **Symbolic "collect star X" goals are a good choice, but define the label as an achievement-set distance and train it by value iteration over the union of all trees.** Build one graph whose nodes are savestates and whose edges are tree edges with frame costs, merged across trees. Run Dijkstra backward from every node that collects X to get exact shortest *in-graph* times. These are much tighter than per-tree earliest-descendant labels because they cross between trees wherever states match. This is what DeepCubeA-style approximate value iteration does in a learned form ([Agostinelli et al. 2019](https://deepcube.igb.uci.edu/static/files/SolvingTheRubiksCubeWithDeepReinforcementLearningAndSearch_Final.pdf)). Use exact matching only for the graph's discrete layer (star set and course); the learned d supplies the "soft" cross-tree edges (point 6).
5. **Expert iteration loop with a frozen-eval protocol.** Each search round produces better routes, the model is retrained on the union, and the new model guides the next search: ExIt ([Anthony et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)) and bootstrap-learned heuristics ([Jabbari Arfaee, Zilles, Holte 2011](https://ifp.nyu.edu/2011/journal-article-abstracts/learning-heuristic-functions-for-large-state-spaces/)). The bootstrap literature's main lesson is to train on the instances the current heuristic *can* solve, then raise the difficulty.
6. **Landmark graph plus learned local distance for long range (SoRB/SPTM pattern).** Trust d only below ~300 frames, where it is already excellent. Build a sparse graph of savestate landmarks with edges where d < threshold, run shortest paths, and use the graph distance as h. Long-range accuracy then comes from search, not from the network ([Eysenbach et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05253), [Savinov et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00653)).
7. **Fix the 2-5x underestimate with calibration, not by abandoning the lower-bound flavour.** For weighted A*, an h that is consistently low by a constant factor is harmless, since the weight w absorbs it. Rank errors are what hurt. Calibrate with isotonic regression of predicted against TAS-true distance on held-out TAS segments, and evaluate ranking separately from scale.
8. **Train the heuristic on what search actually queries.** Learned heuristics fail when the frontier distribution drifts away from the training distribution. Log every node the weighted-A* expands and every RRT start candidate, and add those states (with TD or graph labels) to training each iteration. This is the core of ExIt and AlphaZero-style data generation ([Silver et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815)).
9. **Pair the heuristic with a policy prior: PHS or LevinTS-style search.** Our GA attempts are a policy. Policy-guided heuristic search ([Orseau & Lelis 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11505)) gives a search-cost guarantee that depends on both policy and heuristic quality, and its learning loop is bootstrap-style. A cheap version: priority = `g + w*h - c*log pi(attempt)`.
10. **Evaluate with route-held-out splits and search-in-the-loop metrics.** Report Spearman by distance bucket, on unseen trees *and* unseen stars or courses. The decisive metric is nodes expanded or frames-to-star when the model is used as h, compared with a baseline h.
11. **Sharpen the goal embedding with dual or temporal-distance-defined goal representations.** Defining a goal by its distances to other states gives sufficiency and noise invariance ([Park et al. 2025, dual goal representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06714)). For star goals, initialise each star embedding from the mean phi(s) of the states that collect it.
12. **Keep the QRL "push apart" term but restrict it to pairs known to be far.** Random pairs from different courses or star sets are safe negatives. Random pairs inside one tree may in fact be close, so push them only up to the TD/graph target, never past it. This keeps QRL's maximisation from inflating d where the local constraint is loose.
13. **Use the encoder design that already works; spend compute on data, not size.** Entity transformers over typed tokens are the AlphaStar/OpenAI Five pattern and are adequate. Bigger models do not fix horizon problems ([Park et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04168)).

## 1. Temporal distances and goal-conditioned values

- **QRL**: Wang, Torralba, Isola, Zhang 2023, [arXiv 2304.01203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01203). Learns a quasimetric d(s,g) by maximising the expected distance of random pairs, under the constraint that each observed transition costs at most one step (squared-hinge penalty with a Lagrange multiplier) **[objective details from memory]**. Because the triangle inequality is built in, local constraints propagate to optimal long-range distances. On suboptimal data this gives the *shortest* path in the data graph rather than the behaviour-policy time, which is the property we need. Our current setup has QRL's push-apart term but *regresses on long MC labels* instead of relying on local constraints.
- **IQE**: Wang & Isola 2022, [arXiv 2211.15120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15120). Interval quasimetric embeddings are a universal approximator of quasimetrics with good inductive bias **[from memory]**. **MRN**: Liu et al. 2022, [arXiv 2208.08133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08133), a metric plus asymmetric max residual.
- **TMD (Temporal Metric Distillation)**: Myers, Zheng, Eysenbach, Levine 2025, [arXiv 2509.20478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20478). Combines a contrastive (successor-style) objective with quasimetric invariances, enforced by an MRN parameterization, to learn *optimal* goal-reaching distances from suboptimal data, including in stochastic environments. Gains are largest on long-horizon stitching tasks **[verified abstract]**. The authors note IQE could be a more expressive choice.
- **Multistep quasimetric learning**: Zheng, Myers, Eysenbach, Levine 2025, [arXiv 2511.07730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07730). Fits a quasimetric with a multistep Monte-Carlo return, which the authors describe as combining TD's local, optimality-preserving updates with MC's global multi-step updates. Reported to work on tasks up to 4,000 steps, including visual ones, and to show horizon generalization **[verified abstract; exact loss not checked]**. This is the closest match to our "MC upper bounds plus local consistency" situation.
- **CMD**: Myers et al. 2024, "Learning Temporal Distances: Contrastive Successor Features Can Provide a Metric Structure", [arXiv 2406.17098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17098). Turns contrastive successor features into a quasimetric distance **[from memory]**.
- **Horizon generalization**: Myers, Ji, Eysenbach 2025, [arXiv 2501.02709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02709). Connects generalization from near to far goals with planning invariance (a policy toward g should act as it would toward a waypoint on the way). Argues quasimetric methods already exhibit this generalization **[verified]**.
- **Contrastive RL**: Eysenbach et al. 2022, [arXiv 2206.07568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07568). An InfoNCE critic over (s,a) and future states sampled geometrically is a goal-conditioned Q-function, proportional to the discounted occupancy. It is MC-style, so on suboptimal data it estimates the behaviour policy's occupancy unless paired with policy improvement. **C-learning**: Eysenbach, Salakhutdinov, Levine 2020, [arXiv 2011.08909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08909), the TD (bootstrapped) classifier version, which can estimate the occupancy of a *different* (improved) policy **[from memory]**.
- **HIQL**: Park, Ghosh, Eysenbach, Levine 2023, [arXiv 2307.11949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11949). Learns one action-free goal-conditioned value with IQL-style *expectile TD*, V(s,g) toward r + gamma V(s',g), which approximates the in-sample best path. It then extracts a high-level policy that proposes subgoal representations about k steps ahead, plus a low-level policy. Motivation: values far from the goal are noisy, so the signal-to-noise ratio is better for nearby subgoals **[from memory]**. **This is the most direct template for our "expectile on Bellman target" change.**
- **HILP**: Park, Kreiman, Levine 2024, [arXiv 2402.15567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15567). Learns phi so that ||phi(s) - phi(g)|| equals temporal distance, trained with expectile TD (symmetric, Hilbert-space metric) **[from memory]**. **TLDR**: Bae, Park, Lee 2024, [arXiv 2407.08464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08464). Uses temporal-distance representations to choose far goals and reward exploration inside a Go-Explore loop **[verified]**.
- **Dual goal representations**: Park, Mann, Levine 2025, [arXiv 2510.06714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06714). Represents a goal by its temporal distances to all states, which gives sufficiency and noise invariance. A plug-in for GCRL methods **[verified]**.
- **METRA**: Park et al. 2023, [arXiv 2310.08887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08887). Unsupervised skill discovery in a latent space where one step has bounded temporal distance (a Wasserstein-style constraint) **[from memory]**. Useful as an exploration bonus idea, less so for our heuristic.
- **Successor features / SR**: Barreto et al. 2016, [arXiv 1606.05312](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312). Policy-dependent, so they carry the same suboptimality caveat as MC labels.
- **Dynamical Distance Learning**: Hartikainen et al. 2019, [arXiv 1907.08225](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08225). Regresses MC time-to-reach on on-policy data. The authors note this yields *policy-dependent* distances, which is the same issue as our tree-route labels **[from memory]**.
- **GCSL / HER**: [Ghosh et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06088) and [Andrychowicz et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495). Hindsight relabelling, the source of our pair sampling.
- **Navigation distance plus graphs**: SPTM, [Savinov et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00653) (a binary "close within k steps" classifier, a memory graph, then shortest path); SoRB, [Eysenbach et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05253) (distributional goal-conditioned critic for local distances, Dijkstra over replay-buffer states for long range); Sparse Graphical Memory, [Emmons et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06417) (graph sparsification by two-way consistency); ViNG, [Shah et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09812), and RECON, [Shah et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05859) (learned temporal distance used for graph edges and pruning); NoMaD, [Sridhar et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07896) (diffusion policy plus a temporal-distance head inside a topological graph). The consistent lesson is that **learned distances are trusted only locally, and long range comes from graph search.** SoRB's distributional critic was specifically chosen to be robust at the threshold **[from memory]**.
- **Go-Explore**: [Ecoffet et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995) and [Nature 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919). An archive of "cells" (downscaled frames or domain RAM features such as room and level), return to a cell via a restored emulator state, then explore. Selection weights favour cells that are rarely visited or promising. Our RRT-with-savestates is the same family. The "robustify" phase (backward algorithm from demonstrations) is analogous to distilling a TAS into a policy.

## 2. Learned heuristics for search

- **DeepCubeA**: Agostinelli, McAleer, Shmakov, Baldi 2019, [Nat. Mach. Intell.](https://deepcube.igb.uci.edu/static/files/SolvingTheRubiksCubeWithDeepReinforcementLearningAndSearch_Final.pdf). Deep Approximate Value Iteration trains the cost-to-go by one-step Bellman min over all successors, `J(s) <- min_a [1 + J_target(A(s,a))]`, on states scrambled backward from the goal (a curriculum by scramble depth). It is used in batch-weighted A*, finds the shortest path 60.3% of the time, and solves all test cubes **[verified via paper PDF summary]**. The key ingredients are a Bellman *min* target, a target network, and a near-to-far curriculum. Follow-up: Q* search, ["A* Search Without Expansions: Learning Heuristic Functions with Deep Q-Networks"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04518). A Q-network scores all children in one pass, so the search generates one node per iteration. The authors report up to 129x faster search and 1288x fewer generated nodes than A* **[verified]**. This is relevant if h evaluations dominate our weighted-A* cost.
- **Bootstrap learning of heuristics**: Jabbari Arfaee, Zilles, Holte 2011, [AIJ](https://ifp.nyu.edu/2011/journal-article-abstracts/learning-heuristic-functions-for-large-state-spaces/), and the [SoCS 2010 version](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~holte/Publications/SoCS2010.pdf). Starts from a weak h0, solves what it can with a time limit, trains on those solution costs, and repeats on harder instances. Near-optimal results on the 24-puzzle, with the cost that bootstrapping takes days. [Lelis et al. 2012](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~santanad/papers/2012/lelisJAZH12.pdf) speeds this up with predicted solution costs **[verified summaries]**. Solution costs from suboptimal search are upper bounds, as ours are. They still worked as training targets because each iteration's solutions get closer to optimal.
- **LevinTS / PHS**: [Orseau, Lelis, Lattimore, Weber 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10928) and [Orseau & Lelis 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11505). Best-first search using a policy (LevinTS: cost = depth / pi(path)) or policy plus heuristic (PHS). Search-effort bounds depend on policy and heuristic quality. Training runs in a bootstrap loop on solved instances. PHS* solved all 1,000 test 10x10 Sokoban instances **[verified]**.
- **Sokoban curriculum**: [Feng, Gomes, Selman 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02689) solves hard Sokoban with curriculum-driven deep RL **[from memory]**.
- **AlphaZero / MuZero**: [AlphaZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815) value targets are game outcomes z under the improved (MCTS) policy, so the targets improve as search improves. [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265) uses n-step bootstrapped value targets from the search value. Both are instances of training on search-improved data.
- **Expert Iteration**: [Anthony, Tian, Barber 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439). The expert is tree search guided by an apprentice network, and the apprentice imitates the expert's choices and values. Our "search results feed the next model" plan is exactly ExIt.

## 3. Set / object-centric encoders and RAM state

- **Deep Sets** [1703.06114](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06114), **Set Transformer** [1810.00825](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00825) (induced-point attention, PMA pooling), **Perceiver** [2103.03206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03206) (a latent array cross-attends to large inputs, which would cheaply scale the triangle count), **Slot Attention** [2006.15055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15055).
- **AlphaStar**: [Vinyals et al. 2019, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z). A transformer over up to roughly 512 entity tokens, with scatter connections into a spatial map and pointer-network action heads **[from memory]**. **OpenAI Five**: [Berner et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680). Per-unit embeddings plus max-pooling and a large LSTM. Our typed-token transformer follows the same pattern. One AlphaStar-style trick worth copying is the *pointer head* for "which object matters for goal X".
- **RAM states**: [Sygnowski & Michalewski 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01335) learned Atari from the 128-byte RAM. AtariARI, [Anand et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08226), labels RAM state variables for probing representations. OCAtari, [Delfosse et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08649), extracts object-centric states from RAM. These support structured RAM tokens over pixels, as we do.

## 4. SM64 / TAS-adjacent prior work

- **learnfun/playfun** (Tom Murphy VII, SIGBOVIK 2013, [paper PDF](https://www.gwern.net/docs/www/www.cs.cmu.edu/c9a6be6c3c68b0ef2142baa2c1d8e6eb74604b50.pdf)). Learns lexicographic orderings over NES RAM from a human playthrough, then plays by savestate search ("time travel") to make those orderings increase. It finished some levels and got stuck on long jumps that needed deep lookahead. The closest spiritual ancestor to RAM objectives plus savestate search; its failure mode was myopic objectives that ignore long horizons.
- **sm64_bruteforcers** ([FramePerfection](https://gh.r-mj.com/FramePerfection/sm64_bruteforcers)). Standalone simulators of SM64 portions, used to improve existing .m64 TAS input files with JSON-configured state **[unsure of the search algorithm used]**. Community TAS practice (STROOP, Mupen Lua, bruteforcing) is local optimization around an existing route, consistent with "the TAS gives tight pairs".
- **gumbo64/sm64-ai** ([GitHub](https://github.com/gumbo64/sm64-ai)). Python bindings to the decompiled SM64 (faster than emulation), multi-agent, PettingZoo/CleanRL. Curiosity-based exploration worked best **[from README via search summary]**.
- **Kaze Emanuar's 2018 SM64 AI** ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/ai-learns-to-play-mario-64-gets-a-star-1823170045)). Needed a simplified local world view and only occasionally got a star.
- **N64 RL tooling**: [gym-mupen64plus](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fbzier%2Fgym-mupen64plus); the [SSB64 RL write-up](https://wulfebw.github.io/post/ssb64-rl-01) notes variable emulator timing.
- I found **no peer-reviewed work** on learned time-to-goal heuristics for SM64 or TAS route search. This appears to be an open niche.

## 5. Recommendations for our setting

**Long-range distances**
- *Loss.* Keep the expectile on MC labels as an upper-bound constraint only, i.e. penalise `d > label` more strongly than `d < label`. Add (a) a 1-step local constraint `d(s,s') <= frames(s,s')` (QRL), and (b) an n-step TD target `y = frames(s, s_n) + d_EMA(s_n, g)`, regressed with expectile tau around 0.9 on `y - d`. Expectile over many sampled successors approximates the min across branches (HIQL). Final target: `min(y, MC_label)`.
- *Graph value iteration offline.* Merge all trees into one graph. Exact-equality merges cover star-set or course nodes. Optionally, near-duplicate merging can use RAM position and velocity tolerances or `d < 10` frames in both directions (SGM's two-way consistency). Run multi-source Dijkstra from star-X collection nodes and use the result as labels. This is cheap and gives far tighter labels than per-tree earliest-descendant.
- *Distributional head.* For upper-bound data, predict quantiles of log-frames and use a low quantile (q of 0.1-0.2) as h. Because labels upper-bound the truth, low quantiles better approximate the optimum. A SoRB-style distributional critic gives a robustness precedent.
- *Curriculum.* Use near-to-far, as DeepCubeA does with scramble depth: weight pairs so each log-distance bucket is equally represented, and anneal the max horizon up as TD errors converge.
- *Hierarchy.* For A*, use h = graph distance through landmarks (subgoals whose star set is a superset, or HIQL-style k-step subgoal representations), with the network used only for edges under ~300 frames.

**Goal definition**
- Prefer symbolic goals ("star X", optionally plus a course-entry state) for search. State goals stay useful as an auxiliary task: they shape phi and cover the TAS. Give each star a learned embedding initialised from the phi of its collection states (dual representations), with the IQE distance computed between phi(s) and psi(X).
- *Superset rule.* d(s, X) = infinity (masked) if X is unreachable from the state's star set (for example star-door requirements). Train the reachability head on that rule and do not regress d there.
- Condition on the progress counters explicitly. Distances across different star sets are often unrelated, so make the star set a token, which the model already has.

**Evaluation**
- Splits: held-out trees, held-out stars, and the TAS. Report Spearman and Kendall by true-distance bucket (<300, 300-3k, >3k frames), plus calibration slope.
- The search-in-the-loop metric is the main one: frames-to-star and expansions for weighted-A* using (a) no h, (b) the current h, and (c) the new h, with equal compute.
- Triangle-violation audit: sample triples and measure `d(a,c) - d(a,b) - d(b,c)` against true graph distances. IQE guarantees the inequality for its own outputs, so check calibration to graph distances instead.

**Pitfalls**
- Pure MC-min labels from one tree carry route bias, which we are seeing.
- Push-apart without local constraints inflates distances. Without a target network, TD bootstrapping can diverge or collapse.
- Distribution shift between training states and search-frontier states.
- Exact-match labelling hides cross-tree shortcuts.
- Overfitting to the single TAS route (the model learns "TAS-ness" as closeness). Downweight TAS pairs in ranking evaluations.
- An over-confident h makes weighted-A* greedy and stuck, so keep an exploration bonus or a diversity term (Go-Explore cell counts).

## Recommended plan

1. **Week 1 (labels).** Build the merged multi-tree savestate graph and compute per-star Dijkstra labels. Compare them with per-tree labels on the TAS (how much tighter they are).
2. **Week 1-2 (loss).** Add the n-step expectile TD loss with an EMA target, the local QRL constraint, and an upper-bound-only MC term. Restrict push-apart to cross-course or cross-star-set pairs. Ablate n in {1, 30, 120} frames.
3. **Week 2 (goals).** Add a per-star goal embedding psi(X), the superset masking, and the reachability head. Keep state goals as an auxiliary.
4. **Week 2-3 (search).** Use h = min over landmarks of `d_net(s, L) + D_graph(L, X)` with d_net trusted below 300 frames, and add a GA-policy prior to the priority (PHS-style).
5. **Ongoing (ExIt).** Each iteration: run search with the current model, log expanded and frontier states, merge them into the graph, recompute labels, retrain (warm start), and evaluate on the frozen held-out set with equal-compute search benchmarks. Stop increasing model size until search-in-the-loop metrics plateau.
